# algomonster vs neetcode: how to choose between text-first pattern drills and free video roadmaps for your interview timeline

Both platforms teach the same underlying skill — recognizing which algorithm a problem is asking for — but they get there in completely different ways, and the price gap between them is bigger than most comparison posts admit. Here's what actually differs, what each one costs right now, and how to decide in about five minutes.

## The short version

NeetCode gives away far more for free. Its roadmaps, the full YouTube explanation library, and the NeetCode 150 list cost nothing, and that free tier is genuinely usable for a first pass at interview prep.

AlgoMonster is a paid product with a thin free tier, and it's built on a specific bet: that you'll learn faster from written explanations, flowcharts and interactive diagrams than from watching someone solve the problem on video. If you're the kind of person who reads documentation instead of watching a tutorial, that bet is aimed at you.

So the practical rule most people land on:

- **On a tight budget or just starting out** → NeetCode's free tier, then decide
- **Interview in 4–8 weeks, know how to code, but freeze on "which approach is this?"** → AlgoMonster's pattern curriculum
- **You learn by watching, not reading** → NeetCode Pro, or the free videos
- **You want to pay once and never think about it again** → compare the lifetime prices below, because the gap is wide

## What each platform actually is

NeetCode started as a YouTube channel and grew into a course platform. The core of it is curated problem lists — NeetCode 150 is the famous one — paired with video walkthroughs hosted on the site. The free tier isn't a teaser; it's the actual content. What Pro adds is workflow: progress tracking, spaced repetition, saved solutions, an in-browser editor, a private Discord, and extra problem sets.

AlgoMonster is a structured course, not a problem bank. The company says it was built by ex-Google engineers and competitive programmers, and the curriculum is organized around 48 coding patterns rather than individual questions. Each pattern comes with a written explanation, a code template you adapt, and practice problems attached to it. There are no videos anywhere on the site — that's a deliberate design choice, not a gap they forgot to fill.

AlgoMonster's own pricing page currently lists 500+ lessons across six courses, 3,000+ editorials and 1,000+ illustrations, plus two bonus courses included with Pro (a CS foundations course and a "build an LLM" course). A specialized Quant Interview Prep course is sold separately. Problem volume is still much smaller than LeetCode's bank, and reviewers on both sides say that's the tradeoff you're accepting: fewer reps, better structure.

## Format: the difference that matters most

This is where the two really diverge, and it's worth being honest about which side you're on before looking at prices.

AlgoMonster expects you to read. A lesson explains the logic, shows a generalized template, then gives you a handful of problems with small variations. The features that stand out are the decision flowcharts — a series of questions like "is the input sorted?" or "are you looking for a contiguous subarray?" that push you toward the right pattern — and **Speedrun**, which runs you through problems as multiple-choice pattern identification instead of full coding. That's the interesting bit: it trains the part of the interview where you sit in silence for ten minutes trying to figure out what the question wants.

NeetCode expects you to watch and then solve. Videos are short and tightly edited, solutions are available in several languages, and the roadmap tells you the order to work through. If you've ever read a written DP explanation three times and still not gotten it, then watched eight minutes of video and immediately understood, you already know which format you need.

One caveat that shows up repeatedly in reviews of AlgoMonster: because everything is text and diagrams, passive reading is a real risk. If you skim the pattern explanation and jump straight to the problem without writing the template out yourself, you'll finish the curriculum with recognition but not fluency.

## Head-to-head on the things people actually ask about

**Price.** For a 3-month prep sprint, NeetCode's free tier or $119 annual is cheaper than AlgoMonster's $99 annual only if you'd otherwise pay for both. For lifetime, AlgoMonster is $189 versus NeetCode's $297 — a $108 difference for permanent access.

**Curriculum structure.** AlgoMonster's 48 explicit patterns with templates and decision flowcharts is more prescriptive. You always know what to study next and why. NeetCode's roadmaps are ordered by topic and difficulty, with the video doing the teaching.

**Progress tooling.** NeetCode Pro wins here — progress tracking, spaced repetition reminders, saved solutions. AlgoMonster has analytics and Speedrun, but the structure is the curriculum itself rather than a review system.

**Languages.** AlgoMonster supports Python, Java, JavaScript, C++, Go and Haskell. NeetCode's videos are primarily Python with solutions in several languages. If you interview in Go or Haskell, that's a real point for AlgoMonster.

**System design.** Neither is a serious answer. AlgoMonster's coverage is described consistently in reviews as introductory, and NeetCode's system design course is for beginners. If you're targeting senior roles where a design round is in the loop, budget for a dedicated resource either way.

**Community and support.** NeetCode has a private Discord for Pro members and a large public following. AlgoMonster has a discussion forum tied to its content. Neither offers mentorship or mock interviews with real engineers.

**What reviewers consistently flag.** AlgoMonster: no videos, relatively few problems, basic system design. NeetCode Pro: much of what you're paying for — the videos and lists — is already free, so the upgrade buys convenience and tracking rather than new teaching.

## Which one should you buy?

**You're months out and money is tight.** Start on NeetCode's free roadmaps. Do 40–50 problems. If your problem is figuring out what to study, you don't need a subscription yet.

**Your interviews are 4–8 weeks away and you keep picking the wrong approach.** AlgoMonster's pattern-plus-template format is built for exactly this failure mode, and unlike a subscription treadmill you can buy lifetime once at $189 and stop thinking about renewals. 👉 [Compare the AlgoMonster plans and current sale pricing](https://algo.monster/subscribe?fpr=li69)

**You learn from video.** NeetCode Pro, without much deliberation. Buying a text-and-diagram course when you learn by watching is how people end up with an unused subscription.

**You want something you'll reuse across several job searches.** AlgoMonster lifetime at $189 is the cheaper way to own this permanently. If you'd rather have the larger video library and the community, NeetCode's $297 lifetime covers future content too.

## Can you use both?

Yes, and it's the setup a lot of successful candidates describe: use AlgoMonster to build the pattern-recognition layer quickly, and NeetCode as the free video backup for any topic that refuses to stick when you read about it. That combination costs $99–$189 total, since NeetCode's videos don't require payment.

The reverse also works. Work NeetCode's roadmap, and if you find yourself solving problems without being able to articulate *why* that approach applies, that's the specific gap a pattern-based course closes.

## FAQ

**Is AlgoMonster cheaper than NeetCode Pro?**
Yes, on both tiers right now. AlgoMonster's annual plan is $99 against NeetCode Pro's $119, and lifetime is $189 against $297. The comparison isn't apples to apples though — NeetCode's free tier removes the need to pay at all in many cases, and its Pro tier adds tracking features on top of otherwise free content.

**Does AlgoMonster have video explanations?**
No. Everything is written text with interactive diagrams, flowcharts and code templates. That's the design, and it's the single most important thing to know before buying.

**Is NeetCode 150 enough on its own?**
For many candidates, yes. It's free, ordered, and covers the high-frequency patterns. What it doesn't give you is a review system or progress tracking unless you pay.

**Which is better for beginners?**
AlgoMonster's free tier is a reasonable preview, but the paid curriculum assumes you can already write working code in one language. If you're still learning what a hash map is, neither platform is the right starting point — get the fundamentals first, then come back.

**Can I get a refund if it doesn't work for me?**
Neither platform advertises a generous trial beyond its free tier, so treat the free content as your test drive and check the current terms on the checkout page before paying.

The honest summary: NeetCode wins on free value and video teaching, AlgoMonster wins on structure, pattern-first training and lifetime price. Pick the one that matches how you actually learn, because both of them work — but only if you finish the curriculum.
